\chapter*{About this sheet}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About this sheet}

This sheet drills vectors and matrix algebra, the linear-algebra toolkit
that the optimization work later takes for granted: vectors and the inner
product, matrix operations, determinants, Gaussian elimination and linear
systems, inverses, rank and linear independence, eigenvalues and
eigenvectors, and quadratic forms with definiteness. Elimination sits in the
middle of that list because everything after it uses it: the inverse is
computed by row-reducing $[A\mid I]$, the rank is the number of pivots, and
the simplex method of the linear-programming volume is elimination with a
pivot-selection rule. The last of these is used throughout the optimization
volumes: every second-order condition in several variables is a definiteness
check on a Hessian.

The structure is the same in every chapter: the \textbf{Core Rules} section
states the rules compactly and says when each one applies; \textbf{Practice
Problems} drills them; \textbf{Solutions} works every problem through in
full. Each chapter closes with a block of \emph{additional problems} for a
second pass through the material.

Work the problems before reading the solutions, and row-reduce by hand. The
point is to make the mechanics automatic, so nothing here needs a
matrix-capable calculator.
